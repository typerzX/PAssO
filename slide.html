Barangay Lamao dominates — and exceeds the municipal total

CH. 08 · KEY_OBSERVATIONS

Key Observations

Signals worth watching from the report

Near-parity with the top-ranked municipality

2nd of 11 — Limay and Mariveles together hold ~81% of Bataan's tax base.

Boom, bust, then stabilization

Peaked in 2023 (₱94.42B), fell through 2025, net −5.1% since 2021.

Extreme industrial concentration

Industrial property alone is 97.3% of taxable AV — minimal diversification.

Unusually large "Others" exemption

78.4% of exempted value falls under "Others" — warrants verification.

Barangay-level data quality issue

Lamao's reported AV alone exceeds the municipal taxable total.

CH. 09 · RECOMMENDATIONS

Recommendations

Priority actions for the next reporting cycle

01

Investigate and verify the "Others" exemption category (₱13.15B, 78.4% of exemptions) to confirm proper classification.

02

Reconcile barangay-level assessed values against the municipal total, with priority on Barangay Lamao.

03

Monitor industrial asset reassessment and depreciation cycles closely, given 97%+ concentration risk.

04

Explore diversification incentives (residential, commercial) to reduce dependence on industrial valuation swings.

05

Conduct a year-over-year variance analysis of the 2024–2025 declines to inform forecasting and budget planning.

Conclusion

High value. High concentration.
High scrutiny needed.

Limay is Bataan's most valuable industrial tax base, nearly on par with Mariveles — but its assessed value is highly volatile and heavily concentrated in industrial assets, with unresolved data-quality flags in both exemption reporting and barangay-level figures.
